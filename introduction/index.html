Introduction

The Global Climate Action Agenda

Climate action by cities, regions and other sub national entities, businesses, international organizations and civil society – known as non-Party stakeholders (NPS) in the UNFCCC process – has been instrumental to the advances made in the past decade towards achieving the goals of the Paris Agreement. Both the appointment of Climate High-Level Champions (CHLC) in 2015 and the launch of the Marrakech Partnership for Global Climate Action (MP) the following year helped kickstart a more structured approach to scaling up the global response to climate change, and enabled a better connection between the Convention and the many voluntary and collaborative actions that were already taking place.

This year, the MP – a framework for continued engagement of all actors involved in implementation of climate action – celebrates its 10-year anniversary

In this new stage, the Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA) is focused on building on the significant progress made over the last 10 years and supporting the implementation of already agreed outcomes from the UNFCCC process, such as the first global stocktake (GST).

Based on the new five-year vision and plan launched at COP 30, the GCAA is now structured under six thematic axes. These axes – covering mitigation, adaptation and means of implementation – restructure the sectoral nature of the MP themes into a framework that more thoroughly reflects the GST outcome.

Each of the axes has a set of objectives, reviewed annually to provide agility and adaptability and to ensure that the priorities of the next CHLCs and their respective COP Presidencies are taken into account.

Accordingly, the first set of key objectives proposed by the COP 30 Presidency has now been revised to reflect feedback from the MP stakeholders and the COP 31 Presidency priorities, including a strong focus on zero waste and circularity as a cross-cutting theme.

Global Climate Action Agenda 2026–2030

The new Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA)

Based on the COP 29 decision to welcome the continuation of the Marrakech Partnership and to continue appointing CHLC for 2026–2030, the Champions of the COP 29 and 30 Presidencies followed the good practice from the previous cycle and developed a new five year-vision for the Global Climate Action Agenda 2026-2030, which was built upon a feedback process informed by dialogue with Parties and NPS throughout 2025.

The GCAA aims at bringing together and mobilizing voluntary climate action by civil society, businesses, investors, cities and other subnational authorities, and countries to intensify emission reductions, climate adaptation and the transition to sustainable economies, as set out in the Paris Agreement.

Activation groups

Under the GCAA 2026-2030, voluntary climate action initiatives are grouped into activation groups, which serve as the operational engine of the renewed MP structure, bringing together initiatives composed of national governments and NPS to catalyse and scale solutions to achieve the specific objectives under each thematic axis.

Plans to Accelerate Solutions (PAS)

With a strong emphasis on implementation, a guiding principle is to build on the existing on-going work by means of Plans to Accelerate Solutions (PAS). Each PAS is a concise, collectively developed multistakeholder workplan identifying priority solutions, key actions, responsibilities and milestones to accelerate implementation by 2028 in alignment with the outcomes of GST 1.

NAZCA portal

Another underlying principle of GCAA 2026-2030 is strengthened transparency of voluntary climate action. Key to this, the Non-State Actors Zone for Climate Action (NAZCA) portal is the main venue through which actors can demonstrate credible progress, highlighting where action is delivering results and where further efforts are needed, and providing greater visibility to the contribution of NPS to the implementation of the Paris Agreement.

Granary of Solutions

Similarly, the 2025-launched Granary of Solutions – developed jointly by the COP 30 Presidency and the CHLC – serves as an overarching repository and knowledge base showcasing case studies, practical routes, and real-world examples of global climate action.

Transparency – NAZCA

From showcasing global climate action to providing evidence of progress

The NAZCA portal was first launched in 2014 by the UNFCCC and the governments of Peru and France as a central tool for building momentum and support towards the adoption of the Paris Agreement. Today, NAZCA serves as the central transparency hub for voluntary climate action by NPS, tracking individual climate action by businesses, organizations, cities, subnational governments and regions, as well as collective forms of climate action.

A live directory of climate commitments from countries, cities, businesses and international coalitions, NAZCA aims not only to recognize actors and encourage greater ambition and engagement but also to contribute to the transparency of these voluntary actions.

In particular, the portal tracks the progress of Cooperative Climate Initiatives (CCIs), including initiatives participating in the activation groups, whose information is collected through voluntary registration.

Within NAZCA, CCIs provide the main framework for tracking collective climate action. Defined as voluntary institutional arrangements jointly organized by multiple entities, including Parties and NPS, CCIs include multi-stakeholder partnerships, joint declarations, coalitions, alliances, and other types of voluntary arrangements where multiple organizations work together towards achieving a clearly defined climate-related goal, aligned with the goals of the Paris Agreement and shared by the entities within the CCI. Through a common reporting framework, NAZCA highlights their efforts and progress, including achievements, challenges and opportunities in reaching goals and targets that contribute to the implementation of the Paris Agreement.

CCI progress report

The secretariat invites CCIs registered on NAZCA to report progress annually under a common reporting framework, providing registered CCIs with the opportunity to demonstrate key actions undertaken, report progress on self-identified targets, and share challenges and opportunities encountered. The information reported by CCIs strengthens the credibility of their commitments and helps track their progress, contributing to greater transparency and a better understanding of the collective impact of climate action by NPS.

The growing number of registered initiatives reflects the continued engagement of NPS in voluntary climate action. The secretariat will continue engaging with the global climate action community to encourage initiatives taking climate action to register and be featured in the portal.

Registration, however, is only the first step. Regular progress reporting is paramount to strengthening transparency, demonstrating implementation and understanding how collective efforts are contributing to the goals of the Paris Agreement.

The NAZCA portal: from showcasing global climate action to providing evidence of progress

Since its inception, the NAZCA portal has evolved significantly, improving usability and expanding the breadth of NPS information to include commitments, inventories, plans, and climate actions and tracking NPS progress towards disclosed targets.

As part of its latest evolution, the NAZCA portal aims to transform fragmented data and information into coherent, credible and actionable data that can support real-economy transformation. Strategically, NAZCA will align with the upcoming UNFCCC Climate Data Hub architecture, with the aim to ultimately represent the single, centralized, and authoritative source of global climate data and information to inform policymakers, stakeholders, and the public, supporting multilateral climate change processes, climate actions, and transparency. At its core, the portal aims to provide the means for global climate action to demonstrate evidence of progress, thus ensuring credibility.

In addition to enhancing the data utility of the portal, the newly established NAZCA Network engagement model aims to bring climate action information and analysis directly to stakeholders, while also leveraging their feedback to inform continuous improvements to the public-facing portal.

Building on its role in strengthening the tracking and recognition of voluntary climate action, NAZCA also features the pilot phase of the Net-Zero Recognition Framework (NZRF) announced at COP 30 and launched in February 2026.

Net-Zero Recognition Framework (NZRF) – Pilot Phase

Since COP 20, Parties have invited the UNFCCC secretariat to showcase and track commitments and action by non-Party stakeholders (NPS) and strengthen the transparency of initiatives through the NAZCA portal. Building on this role, the secretariat announced the pilot phase of NZRF at COP 30.

The pilot aims to better understand participation processes and test the technical feasibility of showcasing participants’ net-zero journeys through NAZCA.

As part of the pilot phase, NAZCA publicly recognizes net-zero commitments of NPS through Eligible Net-Zero Initiatives (ENZIs) – net-zero Cooperative Climate Initiatives (CCIs) with robust criteria and demonstrated alignment towards the recommendations of the High-Level Expert Group report.

By enhancing the transparency and visibility of participating entities, NZRF aims to recognize and showcase leadership in providing transparency of NPS climate action towards net-zero emissions. It is also intended to promote peer-to-peer learning, encourage wider implementation across the economy and accelerate global climate action. The pilot phase is planned to run through 2026, and its outcomes and the way forward are to be shared at COP 31.

The NAZCA portal and this Yearbook of Global Climate Action are instrumental tools to track and strengthen transparency of progress of implementation of climate action across the GCAA.

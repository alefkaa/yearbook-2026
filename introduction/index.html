Introduction

The Global Climate Action Agenda

Climate action by cities, regions and other sub national entities, businesses, international organizations and civil society – known as non-Party stakeholders (NPS) in the UNFCCC process – has been instrumental to the advances made in the past decade towards achieving the goals of the Paris Agreement. Both the appointment of Climate High-Level Champions (CHLC) in 2015 and the launch of the Marrakech Partnership for Global Climate Action (MP) the following year helped kickstart a more structured approach to scaling up the global response to climate change, and enabled a better connection between the Convention and the many voluntary and collaborative actions that were already taking place.

This year, the MP – a framework for continued engagement of all actors involved in implementation of climate action – celebrates its 10-year anniversary

In this new stage, the Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA) is focused on building on the significant progress made over the last 10 years and supporting the implementation of already agreed outcomes from the UNFCCC process, such as the first global stocktake (GST).

Based on the new five-year vision and plan launched at COP 30, the GCAA is now structured under six thematic axes. These axes – covering mitigation, adaptation and means of implementation – restructure the sectoral nature of the MP themes into a framework that more thoroughly reflects the GST outcome.

Each of the axes has a set of objectives, reviewed annually to provide agility and adaptability and to ensure that the priorities of the next CHLCs and their respective COP Presidencies are taken into account.

Accordingly, the first set of key objectives proposed by the COP 30 Presidency has now been revised to reflect feedback from the MP stakeholders and the COP 31 Presidency priorities, including a strong focus on zero waste and circularity as a cross-cutting theme.

Global Climate Action Agenda 2026–2030

The new Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA)

Based on the COP 29 decision to welcome the continuation of the Marrakech Partnership and to continue appointing CHLC for 2026–2030, the Champions of the COP 29 and 30 Presidencies followed the good practice from the previous cycle and developed a new five year-vision for the Global Climate Action Agenda 2026-2030, which was built upon a feedback process informed by dialogue with Parties and NPS throughout 2025.

The GCAA aims at bringing together and mobilizing voluntary climate action by civil society, businesses, investors, cities and other subnational authorities, and countries to intensify emission reductions, climate adaptation and the transition to sustainable economies, as set out in the Paris Agreement.

Activation groups

Under the GCAA 2026-2030, voluntary climate action initiatives are grouped into activation groups, which serve as the operational engine of the renewed MP structure, bringing together initiatives composed of national governments and NPS to catalyse and scale solutions to achieve the specific objectives under each thematic axis.

Plans to Accelerate Solutions (PAS)

With a strong emphasis on implementation, a guiding principle is to build on the existing on-going work by means of Plans to Accelerate Solutions (PAS). Each PAS is a concise, collectively developed multistakeholder workplan identifying priority solutions, key actions, responsibilities and milestones to accelerate implementation by 2028 in alignment with the outcomes of GST 1.

NAZCA portal

Another underlying principle of GCAA 2026-2030 is strengthened transparency of voluntary climate action. Key to this, the Non-State Actors Zone for Climate Action (NAZCA) portal is the main venue through which actors can demonstrate credible progress, highlighting where action is delivering results and where further efforts are needed, and providing greater visibility to the contribution of NPS to the implementation of the Paris Agreement.

Granary of Solutions

Similarly, the 2025-launched Granary of Solutions – developed jointly by the COP 30 Presidency and the CHLC – serves as an overarching repository and knowledge base showcasing case studies, practical routes, and real-world examples of global climate action.

AXIS 3 / SECTION I

Global Climate Action in 2026 - Insights from the UNFCCC Climate Weeks

The Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA) presents opportunities to advance and accelerate implementation at scale. It is in that spirit that Climate Weeks in Yeosu, Republic of Korea (CW3, held in April) and Baku, Azerbaijan (CW4, September), brought together a broad range of stakeholders – including national delegations, subnational governments and non‑Party stakeholders (NPS) – with a strong focus on showcasing practical climate solutions, including those linked to transforming agriculture and food systems.

Examples shared during the climate weeks illustrated how adaptation action is increasingly being translated into concrete solutions that improve the resilience of food systems. In Cambodia’s Tonle Sap Basin, for instance, climate-smart agriculture initiatives are helping smallholder farmers respond to water stress while supporting ecosystem preservation and food security.

The Fifth dialogue under the just transition work programme, held in Yeosu, focused on just transition pathways for holistic approaches to food security, including on agriculture and ocean-based action. Discussions during the event highlighted experiences relating to decent work, sustainable livelihoods, social protection systems, participatory approaches and skills development, including for smallholder farmers, artisanal fishers, Indigenous Peoples, local communities and workers in informal economies. Participants also exchanged views on gender-responsive and locally led approaches, traditional and Indigenous knowledge, and the role of finance, technology development and transfer, capacity-building and international cooperation in supporting equitable and resilient just transition pathways in the context of food security.

The Implementation Forums organized during CW3 and CW4 were focused on supporting efforts to accelerate climate action by creating a dedicated space where Parties and NPS could meet on equal footing to exchange practical solutions, align priorities, and collectively discuss how to move from ambition to implementation at scale. Under Axis 3, two of the lead high-impact, scalable solutions emerging from the Implementation Labs and Impact Dialogues are described below:

CW3

Regenerative Agroecology by Small‑Scale Farmers

Across Axis 3, the Asian Farmers Association highlighted farmer‑led regenerative practices already delivering adaptation, mitigation and livelihood benefits. These include agroecology, sustainable forestry, fisheries restoration, soil conservation and mangrove reforestation. The emphasis was on knowledge‑based, low‑cost approaches suitable for smallholders facing climate stress.

Rather than introducing new technologies, this solution scales what farmers already practice — if supported by policy recognition, finance and extension services.

Key implementation value

  • Low capital intensity, high resilience benefits
  • Scalable through farmer networks and extension systems
  • Strong adaptation and food‑security co‑benefits
CW4

Build investment-ready pipelines through blended finance and project preparation support

The session identified the gap between agricultural climate solutions and investable projects: countries and farmers often know what needs to be done, but lack the project preparation, risk-reduction mechanisms and financing structures required to attract capital.

Several implementation models were presented, including the Baku Harmoniya Climate Initiative for Farmers and the FAST Partnership. The approaches presented combine public finance, concessional finance, guarantees, insurance, technical assistance, project preparation support and private investment to create bankable pipelines that can unlock much larger public and private investment flows for climate-resilient agriculture.

Key implementation value

  • Reduces investment risk through concessional finance, guarantees, insurance and public support, making agricultural projects more attractive to investors
  • Transforms climate solutions into investment-ready projects through stronger project preparation, MRV systems, technical assistance and investment pipelines
  • Improves access for farmers and SMEs, particularly through blended finance models, differentiated financial products and support mechanisms that can mobilize more capital

Under Objective 8, emissions intensity is improving for some sources but not others. Between 2022 and 2023, FAOSTAT data shows it fell for enteric fermentation and rice cultivation, held flat for soil fertilization and rose for manure management. It also shows crop yields held at 6.8 tonnes per hectare between 2023 and 2024, while ruminant meat productivity rose from 30 to 31 kg per hectare.

Under Objective 9, the share of food production lost held at 13 per cent between 2021 and 2023. No global indicator yet tracks the resilience of food systems to climate shocks or the finance reaching small-scale farmers.

Under Objective 10, the share of people unable to afford a healthy diet fell from 37.4 per cent in 2021 to 32.7 per cent in 2025, though that is still nearly one in three. Undernourishment fell from 9.1 in 2023 to 7.8 per cent of the population in 2025, and 79 per cent of countries legally recognized land tenure rights for Indigenous Peoples and local communities (RRI) in 2020.

AXIS 3 / SECTION IV

COP 31 Action Agenda

Advancing the global transition toward sustainable and resilient food systems through Food Security

Sitting at the nexus of climate, nature and development challenges, agriculture and food systems are central to sustaining the livelihoods of billions. Transforming these systems is essential to achieving the Paris Agreement and advancing outcomes of the first GST relating to reducing food systems emissions along the value chain, restoring land and preventing agriculture-led deforestation, while safeguarding food security and nutrition for all.

Key to achieving this is the plan to advance a food security solutions acceleration workstream complemented by a broader Circular Food Systems and Zero Food Waste component linked to the Zero Waste Package. The efforts here are focused on reducing food loss and waste across value chains, improving organics management, reducing methane emissions and strengthening food security, resilience and nutrition outcomes.

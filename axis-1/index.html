Axis 1 / Section I

Global Climate Action in 2026 - Insights from the UNFCCC Climate Weeks

The Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA) presents opportunities to advance and accelerate implementation at scale. It is in that spirit that Climate Weeks in Yeosu, Republic of Korea (held in April) and Baku, Azerbaijan (September), brought together a broad range of stakeholders – including national delegations and non-Party stakeholders (NPS) – with a strong focus on showcasing practical climate solutions, including those linked to transitioning energy, industry, and transport.

Accelerating renewable energy and clean technology was a central focus of discussions, with recognition that technology alone is not enough. Enabling systems – from modern grids and energy storage to affordable financing, policy certainty, and skilled workforce – are critical to scale up clean energy. Participants, for instance, discussed how small island states can overcome diesel dependency by investing not just in solar or wind farms, but in grid upgrades, storage solutions, and regulatory reforms to handle variable power. In another forum, energy experts noted that renewables have become more affordable than ever and already meet a growing share of global demand, but bottlenecks like slow permitting and lack of grid infrastructure are holding back progress. Heavy industries like steel and cement were discussed not in theoretical terms, but through early-stage projects that illustrate a broader shift to cleaner production. Cities and subnational leaders, particularly from Africa and Asia, shared steps to develop integrated urban mobility systems with public transit improvements and electric vehicles, reducing congestion and pollution while cutting carbon.

The discussions highlighted that tackling systemic barriers – for example, by streamlining approvals, creating incentives for grid investment, and designing just transition pathways for workers – is essential to deliver on the full promise of clean energy for climate and development. It was also highlighted that international and regional cooperation can accelerate the pace of these efforts through joint learning, capacity building, technology innovation and transfer, and the mobilization of finance and investment.

The Implementation Forums organized during CW3 and CW4 were focused on supporting efforts to accelerate climate action by creating a dedicated space where Parties and NPS could meet on equal footing to exchange practical solutions, align priorities, and collectively discuss how to move from ambition to implementation at scale. Under Axis 1, two of the lead high-impact, scalable solutions emerging from the Implementation Labs and Impact Dialogues are described below:

CW3

Scaling Clean Power Supply to Enable Economy-Wide Electrification

The objective is increasingly to elevate electrification as a priority issue within international energy and climate agendas, recognizing its linkages with the global goals to triple renewable energy capacity, double energy efficiency improvements, and transition away from fossil fuels. Discussions also point toward the need for a more coordinated global ambition on electrification, including proposals to collectively reach 35 per cent electrification of final energy use by 2035, while strengthening coordination between governments, industry and investors across planning, policy and implementation.

Key implementation value

  • Proven solutions ready to scale
  • Grid modernization as the critical enabler
  • Strong political convergence heading into COP 31
CW4

Green public procurement and business investment for low-carbon construction materials such as cement, concrete, and steel

Using green public procurement (GPP) creates predictable demand for low-carbon cement, concrete, and steel, thereby giving industry the confidence to invest in cleaner production. By embedding low-carbon criteria into public procurement and procurement guidance, governments can reduce this uncertainty, stimulate innovation, accelerate industrial decarbonization, and help scale low-carbon materials markets.

Examples demonstrated how climate action gains scale when it becomes part of core investment and competitiveness strategies.

Key implementation value

  • Creates demand certainty for industry, enabling producers to invest with greater confidence
  • Uses existing public spending rather than new subsidies, allowing governments to shape markets through procurement decisions and infrastructure investments
  • Links climate objectives with industrial development, supporting green industrialization and competitiveness while reducing emissions from hard-to-abate sectors
  • Mobilizes private capital alongside public policy

Key objectives tracked with 26 indicators

Across Axis 1, clean power is now scaling faster than any other part of the energy system, while progress in energy efficiency, decarbonization of heavy industry and the transition away from fossil fuels lag behind.

Under Objective 1, renewable energy capacity increased by 17 per cent from 2024 to 2025, reaching 5,149 gigawatts (GW). A record 108 GW of battery storage was also added in 2025. The 2025 Yearbook forecast that renewables would overtake coal-fired generation by 2026 was reached in 2025, when renewables supplied 33.8 per cent of global electricity against 33.0 per cent for coal (down from 34 per cent in 2024), and solar alone met three-quarters of the growth in demand. Energy efficiency remains the weak point: improvement in energy intensity has slowed to one per cent a year, far from the roughly four per cent needed to double the rate by 2030. Grids remain a critical solution, and there is still no up-to-date global measure of grid expansion.

The highlight under Objective 2 is that road transport is electrifying quickly. Electric vehicles rose from under one per cent of light-duty vehicle sales in 2015 to 25 per cent in 2025, and public charging points from 0.16 million to more than 7 million in 2025. The carbon intensity of steel production stayed flat from last check (2023) at 1,920 kilograms of carbon dioxide (kgCO₂) per tonne, and zero-emission fuels are still close to zero in shipping, though progressing.

Under Objective 3, the number of people without electricity and without clean cooking fell between 2023 and 2024, the latter notably from 2.7 billion to 2 billion, corresponding to a decrease of nearly 26 per cent. However, that also means roughly a quarter of the world’s population still depend on polluting fuels and technologies for cooking.

Finally, under Objective 4, fossil fuels still supply 57.4 per cent of generation in 2025, and oil and gas methane emissions have increased from 71.5 megatonnes of methane (Mt CH₄) in 2024 to around 72.3 Mt CH₄ in 2025. Explicit fossil fuel subsidies fell to USD 0.725 trillion in 2024, while oil and gas production covered by methane pledges fell from 80 per cent in 2024 to ~75 per cent in 2025.

Axis 1 / Section IV

COP 31 Action Agenda

Accelerating access to clean, secure, and affordable energy for all through a Clean Energy Transition and ensuring low-carbon industrial pathways that support development, competitiveness, and just transition through Green Industrialization

The focus is on electrification and distribution, including grids, storage, investment decisions on industrial plants, and energy access for communities. Work under this axis is also geared towards contributing and supporting implementation efforts for the COP 30 Presidency roadmap on transitioning away from fossil fuels in a just, orderly, and equitable manner.

Renewable power has scaled at unprecedented speed but delivery systems (grids, storage, system flexibility, efficient end-use, and clean fuels) have not kept pace. Electrification across industry, transport, and buildings is essential to turn clean power into emissions reductions, accelerating the transition away from fossil fuels and increasing energy efficiency. Grid expansion and modernization are critical enablers, with stronger interconnectors and regional power pool integration.

Likewise, universal energy access remains a structural priority, including through the expansion of distributed renewable energy systems and improved access to clean cooking technologies and fuels.

To ensure progress on green industrialization, bringing governments, businesses and financial institutions into the heart of climate action, with sectoral plans (e.g., steel, cement and concrete, chemicals) is key, as is establishing green industrial hubs and corridors, and green industry support to emerging markets and developing economies. With that in mind, the coordination umbrella provided by the Belém Declaration on Global Green Industrialization will be operationalized by COP 31.

Consistent with the Zero Waste Package, work under this axis is also focused on promoting circular industrial transformation through resource efficiency, industrial symbiosis, circular supply chains, product longevity strategies and the recovery and circular use of critical minerals and materials associated with batteries, renewable energy technologies and electric mobility systems.

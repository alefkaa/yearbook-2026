Axis 2

Stewarding Forests, Oceans and Biodiversity

Axis 2 brings together efforts to halt and reverse deforestation, conserve an restore ecosystems, and preserve oceans are coastal areas. By aligning action across climate, biodiversity and land degradation, it supports healthier ecosystems, stronger communities and a more resilient future.

Key Objectives

Objective 5

Investments to halt and reverse deforestation and forest degradation

Objective 6

Efforts to conserve, protect and restore nature and ecosystems with solutions for climate, biodiversity and desertification

Objective 7

Efforts to preserve and restore oceans and coastal ecosystems

Axis 2 / Section I

Global Climate Action in 2026 - Insights from the UNFCCC Climate Weeks

The Global Climate Action Agenda (GCAA) presents opportunities to advance and accelerate implementation at scale. It is in that spirit that Climate Weeks in Yeosu, Republic of Korea (held in April) and Baku, Azerbaijan (September), brought together a broad range of stakeholders – including national delegations and non‑Party stakeholders (NPS) – with a strong focus on showcasing practical climate solutions, including those linked to stewarding forests, oceans and biodiversity.

During these events, country examples illustrated how adaptation action is increasingly being translated into concrete solutions that reinforce the resilience of oceans and coastal areas, among others, delivering broad-based positive outcomes. In Bangladesh, for instance, integrated coastal resilience initiatives are combining livelihood diversification, water security and community capacity-building to reduce vulnerability in climate-exposed areas. Through local success stories – like Nepal’s community forestry program (where 23,000 local groups have restored millions of hectares of forest) – climate weeks illustrated that empowering communities can deliver big wins for both climate and livelihoods.

The Implementation Forums organized during CW3 and CW4 were focused on supporting efforts to accelerate climate action by creating a dedicated space where Parties and NPS could meet on equal footing to exchange practical solutions, align priorities, and collectively discuss how to move from ambition to implementation at scale. Under Axis 2, two of the lead high-impact, scalable solutions emerging from the Implementation Labs and Impact Dialogues are described below:

CW3

Costa Rica’s Payments for Environmental Services (PES) Across Land and Sea

Costa Rica showcased a mature and adaptable payments for environmental services (PES) model, now being extended beyond forests into fisheries, agricultural landscapes, and sustainable branding (“Planet Avo”). By rewarding producers and resource managers directly for ecosystem stewardship, Costa Rica demonstrates how nature protection, livelihoods and markets can be aligned.

The solution goes beyond conservation by embedding incentives across value chains, creating both environmental and economic returns.

Key implementation value

  • Proven national model with decades of experience
  • Replicable incentive structure adaptable to different ecosystems
  • Directly benefits communities while delivering biodiversity outcomes
CW4

Integrating water across climate planning, policy and investment pipelines through Baku Dialogue on Water for Climate Action

The River Revived Sheki initiative in Azerbaijan combines river restoration, irrigation rehabilitation, ecosystem protection, cultural heritage preservation and sustainable tourism development within a single implementation framework, demonstrating how water investments can simultaneously strengthen resilience, livelihoods and local economic development.

Building on examples such as Sheki, the Baku Dialogue on Water for Climate Action is developing a work programme around three workstreams, through which the Dialogue is intended to move countries from commitments to implementation by supporting stronger integration of water into NDCs, NAPs and investment pipelines while providing continuity across COP cycles.

Key implementation value

  • Creates a permanent coordination mechanism that brings together different stakeholders around country implementation needs
  • Improves policy coherence by integrating water into NDCs, NAPs, adaptation planning and broader climate strategies
  • Connects evidence, finance and delivery, helping translate water priorities into investable projects, stronger institutions and practical actions on the ground

Across Axis 2, progress is evident in expanding finance, conservation efforts and recognition of Indigenous and community rights. However, these advances have not yet reversed the continuing degradation of forests and ecosystems.

Under Objective 5, the data show that deforestation slowed in 2025, but forest degradation is still rising. Forests still receive less than one per cent of global climate finance representing an estimated USD 66.8 billion annual finance gap.

Under Objective 6, there are now 11.8 million hectares under active restoration projects, up from 10.6 Mha in 2024. No global indicator yet tracks ecosystem integrity, biodiversity recovery, long term effectiveness or desertification, a gap the COP 31 focus on Rio Synergies makes more pressing.

Under Objective 7, marine protected areas have more than doubled in the past decade, from 4.1 per cent of the ocean in 2015 to 10 per cent in 2025, still well short of the 30 per cent goal for 2030. Global Mangrove Watch now estimates mangrove extent at around 150,000 km² (2025), but a change in methodology means this cannot be compared with the earlier estimate of about 137,000 km² (2020).

Axis 2 / Section IV

COP 31 Action Agenda

Integrating solutions where climate, biodiversity, and desertification converge through Rio Synergies and protecting the Ocean and Seas

As regions around the world confront a multidimensional environmental crisis where drought, water scarcity, land degradation, and wildfires reinforce one another, the COP 31 Action Agenda stresses the need for integrated approaches and solutions where climate, biodiversity, and desertification converge. This approach seeks to align the objectives of the three Rio Conventions (UNFCCC, the Convention to Combat Desertification – UNCCD – and the Convention on Biological Diversity – CBD) into a single, mutually reinforcing line of on-the-ground action.

The year of 2026 also marks a triple COP cycle for the Rio Conventions, with UNCCD and CBD also convening before UNFCCC’s COP 31. UNCCD’s COP 17 was held in August, in Mongolia, under the theme Restoring Land. Restoring Hope. CBD held its COP 17 in October, in Armenia, marking the first global review of collective progress towards the implementation of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF).

Explainer — Rio Conventions synergies

The Rio Conventions work hand in hand to align efforts on ecosystem restoration, sustainable land management and nature-based climate solutions in recognition of the need for an integrated response to address these interconnected challenges.

Strengthening synergies across policies, data, capacity-building and science ensures that actions in one area support and accelerate progress in others. Joint work enhances national implementation, fosters inclusive governance and advances shared global Sustainable Development Goals. Integrated solutions that tackle climate change, biodiversity loss and land degradation together include:

  • Nature-based solutions (NbS) – protecting and restoring ecosystems to absorb carbon, conserve biodiversity and enhance resilience – and Ecosystem-based adaptation (EbA) – using natural systems to help communities adapt to climate impacts
  • Sustainable land use – ensuring agriculture, forestry and other land uses maintain ecosystem health

Ocean & blue carbon priority

Another priority of the COP 31 GCAA is protecting marine ecosystems and unlocking blue carbon for climate resilience. The aim is to strengthen ocean-climate action through enhanced observation, data sharing, and regional cooperation. Given the oceans' critical foundation for resilience, food security, livelihoods and economic stability, scaling ocean-based solutions can help translate global ambition into place-based delivery and strengthen the role indigenous people, coastal communities and entrepreneurs as actors of change and solution leaders.

Zero Waste Package

The Zero Waste Package also aims at supporting efforts to reduce pollution pressures on terrestrial and marine ecosystems, recognizing the contribution of circular economy and zero waste approaches to ecosystem restoration, biodiversity conservation and the reduction of waste leakage into rivers and oceans.
